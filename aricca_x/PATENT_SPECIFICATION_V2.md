## 2. Technical Problem Statement (Expanded)

### 2.1 Why Predatory Venue Detection Alone Is Insufficient

The academic integrity crisis extends far beyond fake conferences. A 2024 report by the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE) identified **seven interconnected threat vectors** that no single tool currently addresses:

1. **Predatory Venues** -- Fake conferences/journals that collect fees without legitimate peer review
2. **Paper Mills** -- Organized operations that mass-produce fraudulent manuscripts
3. **Citation Cartels** -- Groups of authors who systematically inflate each other's citation counts
4. **AI-Generated Reviews** -- Fabricated peer reviews generated by LLMs to game the review process
5. **Reviewer Manipulation** -- Authors suggesting fake reviewers using fabricated email addresses
6. **Grant Compliance Violations** -- Researchers publishing in venues that violate funder mandates
7. **Identity Fraud** -- Researchers falsifying institutional affiliations, ORCID records, or co-author lists

ARICCA-X is the first platform to address **all seven threat vectors** through a unified, deterministic architecture.

### 2.2 Why Deterministic Processing Is a Competitive Moat

| Approach | Failure Mode |
|----------|-------------|
| **ML Classifiers** | Non-reproducible outputs - institutions cannot legally justify decisions based on them - liability risk |
| **Manual Curation** | Cannot scale - Beall's List was shut down due to legal threats - single point of failure |
| **ARICCA-X (Deterministic)** | Every output is mathematically reproducible - provides audit trail - legally defensible - no single curator dependency |

### MODULE B: Researcher Auditing Engine (New -- Claims 6-8)

**Use Case**: A journal editor-in-chief needs to verify whether a submitting author's claimed credentials are legitimate before assigning reviewers.

#### CLAIM 6: Deterministic Citation Ring Detection via Bounded Depth-First Traversal

A computer-implemented method for detecting citation manipulation cartels, comprising:

**(a)** constructing a directed citation graph G = (V, E) where vertices represent authors and edges represent citations;

**(b)** performing a bounded depth-first search (DFS) with max depth k=5 to detect reciprocal citation paths;

**(c)** computing a Citation Concentration Ratio (CCR):
```
CCR(v) = max_citing_author_count / total_citation_count
```
Flagging CCR > 0.40 as anomalous;

**(d)** detecting Temporal Citation Bursts where >60% of citations from a single source occur within a 90-day window;

**(e)** aggregating scores using Adaptive Decay Weighting (Claim 1) to produce a composite Citation Integrity Score; and

**(f)** outputting a deterministic CartelRisk classification (NONE | LOW | MODERATE | HIGH | CRITICAL).

**Novelty**: Prior art uses ML-based anomaly detection. ARICCA-X performs deterministic, bounded DFS with explicit ring-size measurement -- producing reproducible cartel risk scores without ML.

#### CLAIM 7: ORCID-Linked Author Identity Verification via Cross-Database Triangulation

A computer-implemented method comprising:

**(a)** extracting author's claimed attributes: name, affiliation, email domain, ORCID, co-author list;

**(b)** querying three independent sources: ORCID API, institutional directories, publication databases;

**(c)** performing Cross-Source Consistency Analysis including Co-Author Network Stability Score (Jaccard similarity over 3-year window);

**(d)** applying Cross-Signal Anomaly Detection (Claim 2) to flag inconsistencies; and

**(e)** outputting deterministic IdentityConfidence classification (VERIFIED | UNVERIFIED | SUSPICIOUS | FRAUDULENT).

#### CLAIM 8: H-Index Inflation Forensics via Self-Citation Decomposition

A computer-implemented method comprising:

**(a)** decomposing h-index into: self-citations, co-author citations, same-institution citations, independent citations;

**(b)** computing Citation Independence Ratio: `CIR = independent / total`;

**(c)** computing Venue Quality Distribution Score by mapping publications to Module A credibility scores;

**(d)** flagging authors where CIR < 0.30 OR venue quality < 0.40; and

**(e)** outputting deterministic MetricIntegrity assessment with decomposed breakdowns.

### MODULE C: Peer-Review Forensics Engine (New -- Claims 9-11)

**Use Case**: A conference program chair suspects reviews were generated by ChatGPT or a reviewer cartel is operating.

#### CLAIM 9: AI-Generated Review Detection via Deterministic Linguistic Fingerprinting

A computer-implemented method comprising:

**(a)** extracting linguistic features: sentence length variance, hedging frequency, specific criticism ratio, lexical diversity, formulaic transitions;

**(b)** computing Specificity Score (references to specific manuscript elements vs total sentences);

**(c)** computing Hedging Density (hedging constructions per 100 words);

**(d)** applying deterministic decision tree:
- Specificity < 0.15 AND Hedging > 8.0 = HIGH risk
- Sentence length CV < 0.20 = elevated suspicion
- Formulaic transitions > 12.0 per 1000 words = elevated suspicion;

**(e)** cross-referencing timestamps against reviewer's historical response-time distribution; and

**(f)** outputting deterministic ReviewAuthenticity classification (AUTHENTIC | SUSPICIOUS | LIKELY_GENERATED).

**Novelty**: Existing AI detectors (GPTZero) use ML classifiers producing non-reproducible scores. This claim uses deterministic feature extraction with fixed thresholds.

#### CLAIM 10: Reviewer Cartel Detection via Assignment-Recommendation Graph Analysis

Detects reciprocal favorable review patterns across submission cycles via bipartite graph analysis with Review Score Deviation measurement.

#### CLAIM 11: Review Sentiment-Content Alignment Analysis

Detects misalignment between review text sentiment and recommendation decision (e.g., negative text but "accept" recommendation).

### MODULE D: Grant Compliance Engine (New -- Claims 12-13)

**Use Case**: A university research office verifies all publications from an NSF-funded project comply with Open Access mandates.

#### CLAIM 12: Deterministic Funding Mandate Compliance Verification

A computer-implemented method comprising:

**(a)** maintaining structured mandate database for Plan S, NIH, NSF, and UKRI policies;

**(b)** for each publication: extracting OA policy, embargo period, license type, repository deposit status;

**(c)** applying 6-Phase Pipeline (Claim 5) to produce per-publication compliance status;

**(d)** aggregating into grant-level compliance report; and

**(e)** generating actionable remediation recommendations.

#### CLAIM 13: Publication-Grant Linkage Verification via Acknowledgment Text Mining

Detects missing attribution (grant-funded work without acknowledgment) and false attribution (publications incorrectly claiming grant funding).
