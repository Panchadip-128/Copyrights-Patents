Dear Inventor, 
We have completed our due diligence under the Computer Related Inventions (CRI) Guidelines 
issued by the Hon. Indian Patent Office, as instructed by Hon. IPTTO MAHE, and we set out our 
observations below in respect of the invention titled "Deterministic Mu lti-Signal Processing for 
Academic Integrity Verification with Missing -Evidence Bounds and Cryptographically Linked 
Phase Records", being a computer-implemented system that evaluates venue, citation, peer-review 
and publication-policy records and outputs a risk status with missing-evidence bounds and hash-
linked phase records. We set out our observations in the hope that they assist you, and we would 
be grateful for your help on the points raised below. 
1. SECTION 3(k) OBJECTION RISK: MATHEMATICAL METHOD, ALGORITHM 
AND COMPUTER PROGRAMME PER SE 
We are of the view that the disclosure, as it presently stands, faces a credible risk of objection 
under Section 3(k) of the Patents Act, 1970. In our assessment, an examiner is likely to find that 
the substance of the disclosure lies in mathematical computation and rule-based logic executed on 
a general-purpose computer, with the output being a score, an interval and a risk tier. 
The disclosure's researcher auditing module enumerates directed citation cycles, partitions 
citations by precedence and recomputes the h -index, CIR and LACIR. Each result is an index or 
a ratio. The review module computes sentence -length variation, hedging density, specificity and 
polarity, and the venue module computes edit distance, a deadline interval and a weighted root -
mean-square fingerprint change. An examiner is likely to treat each of these as a mathematical 
method whose end goal is the number itself.

2. SECTION 3(k) OBJECTION RISK: BUSINESS METHOD 
We are of the view that the disclosure, as it presently stands, also faces a credible risk of objection 
under the business method limb of Section 3(k). The Section 3(k) exclusion for a business method 
is not qualified by "per se", and an examiner is likely to ask where the core of the invention lies. 
The disclosure's grant compliance module selects a publication policy by authority and effective 
date, tests publication metadata such as open-access route, repository deposit, embargo duration, 
licence and funding acknowledgment against that policy, and m aps each failed outcome to a 
remediation code. In our assessment, an examiner is likely to characterise this as the application 
of administrative rules to a record, which is a compliance procedure carried out by computer. The 
disclosure itself states that the sample policies are synthetic and that a failed predicate is a result 
under a chosen rule version and not a legal determination, and an examiner may take that as 
confirming that the module's contribution lies in the rules and not in the machinery. 
The submission gateway receives a submission, assesses it and returns a release, hold or rejection 
code according to a risk tier and an evidence -coverage rule, as claimed. The claimed display

Independence Ratio in the Specification also counts loop-associated citations. 
● The Patent Specification Report flags a review where polarity exceeds 0.6 with a Reject 
recommendation. The Specification flags polarity of at least 0.50 with Reject and of at 
most −0.30 with Accept. 
● The Patent Specification Report gives the formula for hedging density, expressed as a 
percentage of total words, and describes specificity by regular -expression matching, 
without a threshold for either. The Specification gives a hedging threshold of 0.04 a s a 
fraction of word tokens and a specificity threshold of 0.15, and defines the denominators 
of both. 
● The Patent Specification Report states that the bounded search for citation rings runs in 
linear time in the number of authors and citations. The Specification states that no linear 
bound is asserted for complete cycle enumeration, and describes a canonica l minimum-
node start with a recorded truncation status. 
● The Patent Specification Report states 100% cartel identification with zero false negatives 
and processing of 11,500 inputs in under one second. The Specification states a processing 
time of 0.589 seconds, seven verification suites and nine completed examples. The ground 
truth and the cycle lengths covered by the 100% result, and the run to which the 0.589 
second figure relates, were not included in the material we received.
